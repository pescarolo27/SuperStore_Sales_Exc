# Super Store Sales (Excel)

**The Data:** The dataset, sourced from [Hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/15-excel-datasets-for-data-analytics-beginners), provides sales data for a fictional retail company. Spread across three distinct tables, it contains information on products, orders, & customers such as prices, shipping costs, customer details, order & shipping dates, the sales & profits made, product details, the mode of shipment, order priority, & more. The main table contains 1,952 data points, all of which are from customers based in the United States & placed between January-June, 2015. Another table describes details regarding returned items & orders, & a third table has some metadata regarding managers & the region they are based in.  
By analyzing this data, valuable insights can be obtained that can inform the retail company as to trends & patterns regarding their products, their customer base, & their effectiveness of their sales. Such information can be used to monitor key metrics & other key performance indicators & to make more informed decisions when it comes to what adjustments the company could make to increase sales, engagement, satisfaction, & efficiency.

This project was done in May, 2025. In the Excel file, there are seven worksheets containing the following: notes on the dataset, raw versions for each data of the three original tables (labeled with the suffix "_orig"), a cleaner, slightly modified version of one of the tables (labeled with the suffix "_new"), analyses including pivot tables & visuals, & a dashboard containing important metrics & figures ("Dashboard (1)").

### Brief Summary
This project examined information pertaining to sales of retail products & the customers who purchased them. These fictitious orders were made between January-June, 2015 by customers across 49 states in the United States. Various metrics & figures were assembled including the total number of customers, orders, items sold, total & monthly sales & profits, the most popular items & customers, sales across a multitude of factors such as product category & customer region, & more.

The original version of the main dataset (the "Orders_orig" worksheet) contained some small inconsistencies varying from capitalization problems, trailing spaces, one duplicate instance, & some missing values. The extra spaces were removed once the data was tabularized, but the other problems did not affect the main analyses in any way. Ultimately, the duplicate & missing values were not necessarily solvable, & so they were left alone as a result.  
Additional variables were built into the Orders data that indicated the following: how long an order took to process, calculated as the difference in days between the order & shipping dates; the month the order was placed; a variable indicating whether returns had been fulfilled or not (this data was joined from the Returns worksheet); & the manager who oversees orders in specific regions in the US (this data was joined from the Users worksheet).

Across the 1,952 data points, there were 1,130 unique customers who placed 1,365 orders that totaled 25,268 items. The company made about $1,924,300 in sales & $224,100 in profits. On average, customers bought about 19 items per order. Generally, sales & profits peaked in April & June & were at their lowest in March & January. Strikingly, the company only made about $1,000 in profits in January & $103 in March. Overall, sales & profits generally increased across this six-month period.

Sales were also analyzed monthly by product category. Across the six months, sales of items from each category--furniture, office supplies, & technology--typically ranged somewhere between $65,000-$160,000, but sales dipped & exceeded this range a couple of times. Generally, sales of office supplies produced the least sales of the three categories, but they accounted for the greatest monthly total overall (~ $180,000 in April). Sales of technology & furniture items were fairly similar (they never differed by more than about $30,000 per month), but the latter category outproduced furniture items in four of the six months.

The retail company sells products to four distinct segments of customers: Consumer, Corporate, Home Office, & Small Business. In terms of total sales, orders from Corporate customers accounted for the greatest portion, about one-third. Consumers & Small Businesses only differed by about $1,500 in total sales (in favor of the former), whereas Home Office customers accounted for one-quarter of the total sales generated.

Customers were also segmented into four distinct regions across the United States--East, Central, South, & West--each of which was overseen by a particular manager. The Southern region, overseen by Sam, reported both the fewest orders & sales. In particular, the Southern region generated approximately $90,000 less sales than any other region & about $235,000 than the strongest region (the East). Overseen by Erin, the East region reported more than $65,000 in sales than any other region. The East & West regions only differed by four orders, but the former generated about $65,000 more in sales. The Central region, overseen by Chris, reported at least 92 more orders than any other region; however, he reported about $145,000 less in total sales compared to the East.

There were three items that generated more than $50,000 in sales & six that reported at least $35,000. The top four sold between 34-96 total items each. Of these six items, three were technology products & two were office supplies. All three technological items were office machines. The most popular item, an Electric Punch Plastic Comb Binding Machine, was purchased 54 times & amassed about $69,000 in total sales.

A handful of customers were picked out based on their orders. Andrew Gonzalez & Glenda Hunter each made six distinct orders, the most of any customer. Louis Parrish ordered 392 items, the most of anyone. Kristine Connolly generated about $50,475 in sales through her purchases, the most of anyone. Only one other customer generated more than $33,000 in sales. Richard McClure generated about $9,300 in profits, the most of anyone. Only nine customers yielded more than $6,000 in profits.  
Of these five customers, Glenda Hunter returned -$4,000 in profits despite producing over $5,600 in sales. Most of this discrepancy came from a purchase of one Matrix Printer. This may be due to the order being for a single item, having a critical order priority, & being shipped by truck. On another note, three of these five customers are from the Western US.


#### Recommendations
Based on these findings, there are multiple areas in terms of customers & products that are typically more reliable in terms of sales, but there are other factors that warrant investigation & some diagnostic analyses.  
If customers & products that yielded the most sales are more desirable in terms of the value they present for the company, focusing advertising & marketing efforts towards them can potentially bring about greater sales. In particular, investing into more resources towards technology-based & furniture items might yield more revenue than office supplies. Moreover, corporate customers & people from the eastern & western US could be more beneficial in terms of sales than people from the south.  
On the other hand, devising new marketing strategies & techniques could be worthwhile in working to improve sales across other customer segments & regions that were less profitable such as the central & southern US as well as individual consumers & small businesses.

Some of the more negative trends observed encourages the company to drill down into specific details to determine what may have been the cause. For instance, what happened in January & March of 2015 such that only $1,000 & $100 was produced in profits respectively when the other four months generated at least $35,000? Why did the purchase of the Matrix Printer by Glenda Hunter (Order ID 88544) yield -$4,000 in profits? Why did sales in the south region underwhelm compared to the other three regions in terms of total orders & sales? Additionally, why did the central region produce so little in sales on a per-order basis compared to the east & west. Working with the respective managers of these regions, Sam & Chris, could reveal such details & help towards solving them.
